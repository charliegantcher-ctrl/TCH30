How TCH30 works

Membership

TCH30 holds the 30 highest-ranked companies from a 100-stock tech watchlist (semiconductors, AI infrastructure, power and nuclear, space, quantum, robotics, software). Rankings score each company on durability of its 10–15 year role (~30%), growth quality with organic preferred over acquired (~25%), margins and their trend (~20%), free cash flow and balance sheet (~15%), and customer concentration and execution risk (~10%). Share price and valuation play no part in the ranking.

The list is reviewed on the 1st of each month: 10 new companies are researched, the 10 weakest leave the watchlist, and the top 30 is re-ranked. A company enters TCH30 when it ranks in the top 30 and leaves when it falls out.

TCH score

Every stock gets a score out of 1,000. About 70% comes straight from the data and the rest from fixed rubrics. Membership is simply the 30 highest scores.

Weighting

Each member's weight is half its share of the 30 members' total market capitalisation plus half an equal share (1/30). Bigger companies still count for more, but no stock is too small to matter: the largest sits around 11% and the smallest around 1.7%.

Each company page also shows a "business weight" for comparison: its share of the group's trailing-12-month sales, net income (losses count as zero), book value and dividends, equally blended. When a company's market weight is far above its business weight, the market is pricing in a lot of future growth; when it is below, the market is paying less for today's business.

The index started at 1,000.00 on . The prior-month figure shown on the index page is hypothetical: today's weights applied to each member's price change over the month before launch.

Where the data comes from

  • Business figures (revenue growth, margins, free cash flow, customers, retention, backlog): each company's own quarterly earnings release and SEC filings (8-K, 10-Q), or the investor-relations page for foreign companies such as TSMC, ASML and SK Hynix.
  • Stock prices and the index chart: Yahoo Finance daily closes (5 years) and 5-minute prices for the 1-day view, downloaded every weekday after the market closes.
  • Market cap, 12-month sales, net income and book value: financial data sites, refreshed in each monthly update.
  • News and the news part of the score: large, established outlets such as Reuters, Bloomberg, CNBC, The Wall Street Journal and the Financial Times, refreshed weekly.

Keep in mind: earnings releases are written by the companies themselves. They choose which numbers to highlight and often lead with "adjusted" figures that leave out costs such as stock-based pay, so this site uses the official GAAP figures where possible. A few figures (for example SpaceX) come from secondary sources, and the figures for the 11 largest companies were gathered without saved source links, so they are worth checking against each company's own release.

Colors

Green means the figure is positive (growth up, margin above zero, cash flow positive, NRR at or above 100%). Red means negative. Grey means the company does not disclose it or it does not apply, such as NRR for a chipmaker. Operating margin uses the GAAP figure.

Limits

Prices and fundamentals are a snapshot, not live. Quarters differ by company. Figures come from company releases and data aggregators, which companies shape to show themselves well; several figures are computed. This page is for learning and research, not financial advice.
